Wagering requirements explained

A wagering requirement (also called rollover or playthrough) is the total amount you must bet before bonus money turns into a balance you can withdraw. It is written as a multiplier — 30x, 40x, 50x — and the multiplier is applied either to the bonus alone or to the deposit plus the bonus, which changes the real cost of the offer by a factor of two.

The formula

There are only two sums you need.

  • Bonus-only basis: required turnover = bonus amount × multiplier.
  • Deposit + bonus basis: required turnover = (deposit + bonus) × multiplier.

"Turnover" means the sum of all your bets, not the money you have left. If you bet 1 USDT a hundred times you have wagered 100 USDT, however the spins went. A balance of 50 USDT can easily produce 1,000 USDT of turnover; that is the whole point of the rule and the reason rollover sounds worse than it plays.

Here is the same multiplier applied on both bases, using a hypothetical 100 deposit matched 100%:

Bonus only (100)30x3,000
Deposit + bonus (200)30x6,000
Bonus only (100)40x4,000
Deposit + bonus (200)20x4,000

Game weighting: why your progress moves slowly

Most casinos do not count every bet at 100%. Slots usually count fully; table games, video poker and low-edge originals often count at a reduced rate or not at all. If blackjack counts at 10%, a 10 bet moves your rollover bar by 1.

The logic is simple. A game with a low house edge costs the casino little to let you grind, so bonuses either exclude it or weight it down. If the terms list weightings, plan your clearing around the games that count fully — and check whether the originals you actually enjoy are on the list at all. Max bet rules

Nearly every bonus caps the size of a single bet while wagering is active. Break the cap, even once, even by accident on an autoplay setting, and the standard penalty is confiscation of the bonus and anything won from it. This is the single most common reason players lose a cleared bonus at the final step.

Two practical habits: set your bet size manually at the start of a session, and re-check it after switching games, because some clients reset the stake to a game default.

Time limits

Bonuses expire. A seven-day window with a large rollover means you need a real play plan, not an occasional spin. When the clock runs out, the bonus and usually any bonus winnings are removed; your own deposit balance normally stays, but read the terms for the exact behaviour.

Withdrawal locks while wagering is live

Some casinos let you cancel a bonus and withdraw your own money at any time. Others freeze withdrawals entirely until the wagering is finished, expired or forfeited. That distinction matters more than the multiplier if you might want your deposit back mid-session, so check it before you opt in rather than after.

What a bonus actually costs you

You can estimate the expected cost of clearing a bonus with one multiplication: turnover × house edge. On 3,000 of turnover at a 2% edge, the expected loss from the play itself is around 60. Compare that with the bonus you are chasing and you get an honest picture of the offer.

Two warnings about that number. First, it is an average over a very large number of bets, not a prediction for your session — variance dominates over a few hundred spins and can swing the result either way. Second, no bet selection, stake pattern or "system" changes the house edge. Martingale, stop-losses and hot-streak rules alter the shape of your results, never the expectation. Anyone selling you otherwise is selling you something.

Checklist before you click "claim"

  1. Find the basis: bonus only, or deposit plus bonus?
  2. Multiply it out and write the turnover number down.
  3. Check game weighting — which games count, and at what rate.
  4. Note the max bet and keep every stake under it.
  5. Note the expiry date and decide whether you will realistically play that much.
  6. Check whether withdrawals are locked while wagering is active.
  7. Estimate the cost: turnover × the house edge of the games you will play.

If any of those answers makes you uncomfortable, declining the bonus is a perfectly reasonable choice. A plain deposit with no strings lets you cash out whenever you want, which is worth something that no multiplier can express.

FAQ

Does a 30x wagering requirement mean I need to lose 30 times my bonus?

No. It means you must bet that total amount. Wins are recycled into further bets, so a modest balance can generate large turnover. Your expected loss over that turnover is roughly turnover × house edge, which is far smaller than the turnover itself.

Do all bets count equally towards wagering?

Usually not. Casinos weight games differently and often exclude low-edge games entirely. Check the weighting table in the bonus terms before you pick where to play.

What happens if I exceed the max bet by accident?

The normal outcome is that the bonus and everything won from it are removed. Some operators only void the offending bet, but you should assume the strict version and keep stakes well under the cap.

Can I cancel a bonus and keep my own deposit?

At many casinos, yes — forfeiting the bonus returns you to your cash balance. At others, forfeiting is the only way to unlock withdrawals, and any bonus winnings are removed. Read the specific terms; the behaviour is not standard across the industry.

Are low-wagering bonuses always better?

Not automatically. A low multiplier on a deposit-plus-bonus basis, with heavy game weighting and a short expiry, can be harder to clear than a higher multiplier on bonus only. Compare the turnover number and the restrictions together.
