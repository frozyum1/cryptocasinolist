"Provably fair" and "fair payout" are not the same claim

Three different things get bundled under the word fair, and it is worth keeping them apart before you deposit anywhere:

  • Fair results. The outcome was committed to before your bet and can be recomputed afterwards. That is what the seed-and-hash system proves, and only for the games that use it.
  • Fair odds. The payout table against the real probability of winning, which is the house edge. A provably fair game can still carry a high edge.
  • Fair payouts. Whether the casino actually sends the money, how fast, and what it asks for first. No cryptography proves this; the only evidence is published policy and the operator's record.

How provably fair works

Each round's result comes from three inputs:

  • A server seed chosen by the casino. Before you play, the casino shows you only its hash, a fingerprint that commits it to that seed without revealing it.
  • A client seed that you can see and change, so the casino cannot pick a seed that is bad for you specifically.
  • A nonce, a counter that goes up by one with every bet, so each round uses a different input.

The game combines them with HMAC-SHA256 and turns the output into a result: a dice roll, a crash point, mine positions. When you rotate seeds, the casino reveals the old server seed. You can then hash it yourself, confirm it matches the fingerprint you were shown, and recompute every result you played. The commitment is what makes it work: the hash is published first, so the casino cannot swap the seed later and still match the fingerprint you wrote down. A longer walkthrough is in our guide to seeds, hashes and verification.

Verifying a round yourself

  1. Note your client seed and the server seed hash before playing.
  2. Play, then rotate your seed in the fairness settings. The casino reveals the previous server seed.
  3. Check that SHA-256 of the revealed seed equals the hash you noted.
  4. Enter the seeds and nonce in the casino's verifier, or in an independent one, and compare the result with your bet history.

Paste your seeds into our provably fair verifier to run these checks in your browser. Doing it once, on a handful of rounds, is enough to tell you whether the published seeds and the bet history line up. If step 3 fails — the revealed seed does not hash to the fingerprint you were shown — stop playing there.

What provably fair does not cover

  • Third-party slots and live games. These run on the provider's certified random number generator, audited by testing labs, not on seeds you can check.
  • House edge. A provably fair game can still have a high edge. Fair means unmanipulated, not favourable.
  • Withdrawals and account decisions. Proof of fair results says nothing about whether a casino pays out reliably, how long a review takes, or what documents it asks for.
  • Bonus terms. Rollover rules, max bet caps and expiry windows sit outside the fairness system entirely.

Reading the house edge: what a "fair payout" costs you

The edge is the gap between the true odds and what the game pays. On a dice bet with a 50% chance of winning, true odds would pay 2.00x. At a 2% house edge the game pays 1.96x instead, so your expected return is 0.5 × 1.96 = 0.98, or 98 cents back per dollar staked on average. The same logic scales: at a 3% edge you keep 97% of turnover on average, at 4% you keep 96%. These are long-run averages across many bets, not a prediction about your session.

FAQ

Are provably fair games really fair?

They prove the result was not changed after you bet, as long as you check. They do not change the house edge, which is built into the payout table.

Can a casino cheat with provably fair games?

Not on individual results once you can verify them. That is why changing your client seed and checking a few rounds is worth doing once at any new casino.

Are slots provably fair?

No. Slots from external providers use certified random number generators instead. Only casino-built originals use the seed system.

Does a provably fair result mean I will win more?

No. Verification tells you a round was not tampered with. Expected return still follows the house edge: 98 per 100 staked at a 2% edge, 97 at 3%, averaged over many bets.
