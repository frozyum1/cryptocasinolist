How Crash works

Crash is the simplest casino game to understand and the easiest to misunderstand. A multiplier climbs from 1.00x; you cash out any time before it "crashes"; if you are still in when it does, you lose the stake. Here is what decides the number, where the edge lives, and how to verify a round.

Where the crash point comes from

Each round's crash point is computed from a server seed the casino committed to in advance and a public seed such as a block hash or your client seed. The result is a number drawn so that the chance of the multiplier reaching at least m is roughly (1 − edge) / m. The edge is set by the casino and ranges from about 1% to 5%. With a 3% edge, which is what StakeBet runs, the round passes 2.00x about 48.5% of the time, 10x about 9.7% of the time, and 100x about 0.97% of the time.

The multiplier climbing on screen is animation. The crash point was fixed before the round started; the curve just reveals it.

Where the house edge sits

If you always cash out at 2.00x on a 3% game, you win 48.5% of rounds and double your stake each time. Expected return is 0.485 × 2 = 0.97 of your stake, so you lose 3% on average per round. The same maths holds at any target: the edge is the same at 1.10x as at 50x. Choosing a target changes variance, not expectation.

The number to know: the edge is published in each casino's game rules. StakeBet's Crash runs at 3%; its Dice, Mines, Plinko and Limbo run at 2% and Coinflip at 1%. Slots run 3% to 8%. Check the rules page before you assume.

Why strategies don't work

  • Martingale (double after a loss) turns a small edge into a small chance of a huge loss. Ten losses in a row at 2x happen about once every 1,000 sequences, and the eleventh bet is 1,024 units.
  • "Due" multipliers are a fallacy. Each round is independent; five crashes under 1.5x say nothing about the sixth.
  • Cashing out early to be safe lowers variance but not the edge. Cashing at 1.10x wins about 88% of the time on a 3% game and still loses 3% on average.

Auto-cashout and split bets

Auto-cashout removes reaction time from the game, which matters: the difference between clicking at 1.95x and 2.00x is real money at volume. Some sites let you place two bets per round with different targets; that is a variance tool, not an edge.

Verifying a round

  1. Before betting, note the hashed server seed the casino publishes.
  2. After the round, the casino reveals the server seed. Hash it and confirm it matches the commitment.
  3. Run the published formula with the revealed seed and the public seed; you should get the crash point you saw.

Most casinos have a verifier page that does step 3 for you. It proves the outcome was not changed after your bet. It does not prove what the edge is; for that, read the game rules and check the formula.
