The short answer

If you only read one paragraph: take the bonus whose wagering you can finish, on games with a low house edge, inside the time limit — not the one with the biggest number on the banner. A 250% match you cannot clear is worth less than a 100% match you can. And remember that a bonus is an incentive to bet more, not free money; gambling can be addictive, so set the size of your deposit from your own budget before you look at the offer, not after.

The five kinds of casino bonus offers you will see

  • Welcome match. The casino adds a percentage of your first deposit as bonus funds, locked behind a wagering requirement. This is the headline offer at most crypto casinos and the one with the most fine print.
  • No-deposit bonus. A small amount of credit for signing up. Rare at crypto casinos, almost always tiny, and usually carries both high wagering and a low maximum cashout.
  • Free spins. Slot-only credit. The value depends on the spin value and the RTP of the specific slot, not on the number of spins advertised.
  • Rakeback and cashback. A share of your wagering or of your losses returned, typically with little or no wagering attached. Slow but honest: it is a rebate on the house edge you already paid.
  • Reloads, races and leaderboards. Ongoing promotions for existing players. Races pay the biggest wagerers, so for most players the expected value is close to zero.

Welcome match or VIP rewards?

Crypto casinos reward new players in two different ways. A welcome match adds bonus money on your first deposit, locked behind a wagering requirement. VIP and rakeback programmes return a small share of what you wager, usually with no wagering attached, but they only become meaningful once you play a lot.

Some large, established casinos lean on VIP rewards and weekly races rather than a standard first-deposit match; our Stake review goes through what that model means in practice. Newer casinos tend to compete with a bigger up-front match. Neither is a free lunch: the match costs you turnover, and rakeback is a fraction of the house edge you have already paid.

How to read a wagering requirement

A wagering requirement says how much you must bet before bonus money becomes withdrawable. A 30x requirement on a 100 USDT bonus means 3,000 USDT of bets. What that costs you depends on the house edge of the games you play:

GameTypical house edgeExpected cost of 3,000 USDT turnover
Coinflip-style originals1%30 USDT
Dice, Limbo, Mines, Plinko2%60 USDT
Crash3%90 USDT
Typical slot (96% RTP)4%120 USDT

So a 100 USDT bonus with 30x wagering is worth roughly 100 minus the expected cost of the turnover: about 70 USDT on 1% games, and worth less than nothing on a 4% slot, where the expected cost of 3,000 USDT of spins is around 120 USDT. That is an average and variance is large in both directions, but it is the right way to compare offers. Check whether every game counts fully towards wagering; many casinos count originals or table games at a reduced rate, which quietly raises the real cost.

Two details trip people up. First, the multiplier may apply to the bonus alone or to deposit plus bonus — the second version roughly doubles your work. Second, "turnover" counts every bet you place, not money lost, so a balance can cycle through the requirement several times over.

Terms that matter more than the headline

  • Maximum bet while wagering. Breaking it, even once, usually voids the bonus and any winnings from it.
  • Time limit. Seven days for 30x wagering is a lot of play. If you cannot reach the target, the bonus is worth zero.
  • Maximum cashout. Some offers cap what you can withdraw from bonus winnings. A low cap makes a large match pointless.
  • Separate or sticky balance. A separate bonus wallet may let you withdraw your own deposit at any time. A sticky bonus locks your deposit together with the bonus.
  • Game weighting. If live tables count 10% and slots 100%, an "any game" claim is not what it looks like.
  • Withdrawals during wagering. Some casinos pause payouts until the bonus is cleared or forfeited. Look for this line specifically in the terms before you claim — it is the one that matters most if you may want the money back soon.
  • Limited offers. Some welcome bonuses are capped at a number of players; the table shows spots remaining where the casino publishes that figure.

How to compare two offers side by side

  1. Convert both to turnover: match amount × wagering multiplier. That is the number of bets, not the number on the banner.
  2. Multiply that turnover by the house edge of the games you will actually play to get the expected cost.
  3. Subtract the cost from the bonus amount. If the result is negative, the offer is a discount on losses at best, not a gain.
  4. Divide the turnover by your usual bet size and by the maximum bet allowed. If the number of bets does not fit in the time limit at a sane pace, the comparison ends there.
  5. Only then look at extras: cashout caps, weighting, and whether your own deposit stays reachable.

A checklist before you claim

  1. Read the bonus terms page, not the banner. Note wagering, max bet, time limit, max cashout and game weighting.
  2. Work out the expected cost of the turnover on the games you actually enjoy.
  3. Check whether the bonus is sticky and what the terms say about withdrawals while it is active.
  4. Decide your deposit size from your own budget, then see what bonus it unlocks — never the other way round.
  5. If the maths does not clear the bar, decline the bonus. Most casinos let you play without one, and an unbonused balance has no wagering attached to it.

Red flags in a bonus offer

  • Wagering applied to deposit plus bonus, combined with a short deadline.
  • A maximum cashout smaller than the bonus itself.
  • Terms that are not published on the site, only inside the account area or a chat message.
  • Rules that let the casino void winnings for vaguely described "irregular play" without defining it.
  • Any promotion that calls itself risk-free or guaranteed. No casino bonus is either.

There is no single answer, and the table does not rank offers by size — several casinos in it run VIP rewards or multi-deposit packages whose terms vary by region, so a like-for-like comparison of match size is not possible. Do crypto casino bonuses require KYC?

Usually not to claim. Some casinos ask for identity documents before the first withdrawal, bonus or not. Our no-KYC comparison shows each casino's stated policy.

Can I withdraw a bonus straight away?

No. Bonus money becomes withdrawable only after the wagering requirement is met. Whether your own deposit stays reachable in the meantime depends on the casino: some keep bonus funds in a separate wallet, others make the balance sticky or pause payouts until the bonus is cleared or forfeited. Check that clause in the terms before you claim.

What happens if I cancel a bonus halfway through?

Most casinos remove the bonus funds and anything won from them, leaving your remaining real balance. The exact rule is in each casino's bonus terms, so read it before you claim rather than after.
