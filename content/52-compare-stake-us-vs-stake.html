If the site you land on is a sweepstakes product

We are not saying stake.us is one. But most casino-style sites that accept players in US states without licensed online casinos use the social or sweepstakes model, so it is worth knowing how that model works in general before you sign up to anything.

  • Two currencies. One is a play-money currency you buy in packages and can never redeem. The other is a promotional currency that, after being played through, may be redeemable for prizes.
  • No purchase necessary. The model's legal footing depends on the promotional currency being obtainable for free — typically by post or by a daily claim — so buying is framed as optional.
  • Redemption, not withdrawal. You do not "cash out" a balance; you request a prize redemption, which usually needs identity verification and a minimum amount, and often arrives by bank transfer or gift card rather than crypto.
  • State and age limits. Sweepstakes operators exclude certain states entirely, and the excluded list changes. Only the operator's own terms page is authoritative on this.
  • Different odds disclosure. Sweepstakes games are not always provably fair in the cryptographic sense, so you may not be able to verify individual rounds yourself.

Crypto casinos like Stake.com work the other way around: you deposit a coin, you play with that coin's balance, and you withdraw the same coin on-chain. Those are two different products with different rules, different consumer protections and different tax treatment. Do not assume terms from one apply to the other.

How to check the rest yourself in five minutes

  1. Open the site's own Terms and Responsible Gaming pages and find the list of excluded states or countries. Screenshots on forums go stale; the terms page does not.
  2. Find the operator name and licence line in the footer. "No licence listed" is an answer too — it tells you there is no regulator to escalate a dispute to.
  3. Read the redemption or withdrawal section before you deposit: minimum amount, verification requirements, processing window, fees.
  4. If there is a welcome offer, run its terms through our wagering calculator before you accept it. Wagering multiplied by bonus size is the number that matters, not the headline percentage.
  5. Check whether you are on the real domain. Typosquatted copies of big casino brands are a common way players lose access to funds — reach the site from a bookmark or the operator's official social account, not from an ad.
