What makes a casino a "crypto casino"

A crypto casino takes deposits and pays withdrawals in cryptocurrency instead of cards and bank transfers. Your balance is usually held in the coin you deposited, or in a stablecoin such as USDT, and a payout is an on-chain transaction to a wallet address you supply. That single difference drives most of what you see on this page: payouts described in minutes rather than business days, accounts that can sometimes be opened without documents, and in-house games whose results you can recompute from a hash instead of taking the operator's word.

It also changes the risks compared with a card casino. There is no chargeback on a blockchain transaction. Send funds to the wrong address or the wrong network and nobody can reverse it. With no card issuer in the middle, the operator's own terms and track record are what you fall back on if a payout is disputed.

Originals, slots and where the house edge sits

Crypto casino games split into two kinds. "Originals" are simple in-house games that run on a provably fair seed system you can verify round by round. Slots and live tables come from third-party studios: you can read their published RTP, but you cannot verify individual spins yourself.

The house edge is the fixed cut the casino takes on average. Lumenbet publishes these figures for its originals:

GameLumenbet house edge
Coinflip1%
Dice, Mines, Plinko, Limbo2%

A 2% edge means that, averaged over many rounds, 2% of everything staked is expected to stay with the house — so 1,000 USDT of total turnover has an expected cost of 20 USDT, whatever size the individual bets were. That is an average over many rounds, not a prediction for your session. No bet pattern, auto-cashout setting or martingale changes it: raising your stake after a loss changes how your results are distributed, not what they are worth on average. To understand one game properly, start with how Crash works or how Mines works. Keeping a bankroll you can afford to lose matters more than any of it — gambling can be addictive.

What does provably fair actually prove?

Before you bet, the casino publishes a hash of its server seed — a one-way fingerprint it cannot change afterwards. You supply a client seed, and the round's result is computed from both seeds plus a nonce. When the server seed is revealed you re-hash it, confirm it matches the earlier fingerprint, and recompute the result. That proves the outcome was fixed before your bet and not altered after it. It does not change the house edge.

Bitcoin or USDT for depositing?

Bitcoin deposits typically take 10 to 60 minutes to confirm and carry a per-transaction network fee, which weighs most on small amounts. A stablecoin also keeps your balance from moving while you play. See the USDT guide.
